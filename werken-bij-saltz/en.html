The work

What your day looks like

No vague talk about “a dynamic environment”. This is what actually happens between seven in the morning and four in the afternoon.

  • 01

    Picking orders

    You get the order on a handheld scanner, walk your route and scan what you pick. The scanner tells you straight away if something is off.

  • 02

    Getting shipments ready

    Check, weigh, label and put it on the right pallet. What leaves the building is correct.

  • 03

    Loading and unloading

    Unpack incoming pallets and store them, load outgoing shipments. With a pallet truck, or a forklift if you are certified.

  • 04

    Keeping your area right

    Restocking, tidying up, flagging when something is running low. A warehouse that adds up saves everyone time.

Frequently asked

What people usually ask first

I have never worked in a warehouse. Can I still apply?

Yes. Most people here started without experience. You spend your first days with a colleague and work with a handheld scanner that tells you step by step what to pick.

Is my Dutch good enough?

If you understand Dutch or English you will be fine. Both are spoken in the team.

I live in Belgium. Is that a problem?

Not at all — several colleagues cross the border every day. On the phone we explain how tax, social security and applying for a Dutch BSN work if you do not have one yet.

Is this through an agency?

You start with us, and if it works out you get a contract with Saltz Producement itself. In the first call you hear exactly which arrangement applies to you.

Can I work part-time, or only certain days?

Often yes. Put the days and hours you are looking for in the form or your message and we will see if it fits the schedule.

How fast will I hear back?

Within one working day. If you apply over the weekend, we call on Monday.

Still not sure?

Just send a message with your question. It commits you to nothing — and you get an answer faster than you expect.
